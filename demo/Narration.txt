MemoryBox hackathon demo narration

01. Welcome to MemoryBox, a student note organizer. Students create useful notes every day, but those notes often end up scattered, hard to search, and difficult to revisit before exams.

02. A notebook, a phone gallery, chat messages, and a folder full of PDFs split the study workflow. MemoryBox brings these materials into one clear study space.

03. Students can type notes, capture a page with a phone, or upload an image or PDF. The original attachment stays safely beside its saved note.

04. For this biology example, I enter a clear title and subject, then add the note. MemoryBox extracts text, and the student can review it before saving.

05. For handwritten pages, MemoryBox tries to transcribe the image and checks that it contains study material. Students can correct the words; unrelated photos are rejected.

06. The saved note keeps the original image beside readable text. Expand a card to inspect every line; collapse it when you want the notebook to stay tidy.

07. The explanation turns readable notes into a study aid: a simple overview, key points, why the idea matters, and a quick question to practice recall.

08. Search scans titles, subjects, and saved text. A student can find a term such as mitochondria directly, instead of reopening every photo and scrolling through files.

09. Mistakes are easy to fix: delete a wrong upload. For offline study, export the notebook as Markdown or JSON, or print the notes to a PDF.

10. The interface fits laptop and phone browsers, so students can capture notes on the go, then revisit and read them later on another screen.

11. MemoryBox is built with Go and PostgreSQL. A Switch agent adapter can connect a shared Mattermost study room, and summaries use an optional AI provider.

12. MemoryBox keeps the original source, readable text, and study aid together. Capture once, understand better, and find the right note when it matters.
